Achievement Tests vs. Aptitude Tests

Compare

  1. A final exam in a Statistics course — measures how well students have mastered material that has already been taught.
  2. An Academic Potential Test (TPA) for admission to a master’s program — measures how likely a prospective student is to succeed in further study, before that study begins.

Both are cognitive tests that measure maximal performance. The difference: (1) measures learning outcomes that have already occurred, (2) measures the capacity for learning that has not yet occurred. This distinction was introduced in Meeting 1 — today we go deeper into point (2).

Definition of an aptitude test

Definition

Definition

An aptitude test is a measurement tool designed to estimate a person’s capacity to learn a particular skill or field in the future (Anastasi & Urbina, 1997).

  • An aptitude test does not assume the test-taker has formally studied the material being tested.
  • Aptitude test scores are interpreted as predictions of future performance.

Azwar (2019).

Aptitude test vs. achievement test

Comparison

Aptitude Test Achievement Test
Measures Future learning capacity Mastery of material already taught
Time orientation Predictive Retrospective
Dependence on a specific curriculum Low High
Example uses Admissions, job placement School exams, certification
Quality evaluation criterion Predictive validity Content validity

Adapted from Anastasi & Urbina (1997) and Azwar (2019); see also Meeting 1.

The line between the two is not always clear

  • An aptitude test still requires a certain amount of prior knowledge as a medium of measurement — a quantitative item, for instance, still uses arithmetic operations the test-taker has already learned.
  • Because of this, some experts note that a strong correlation between achievement test scores and aptitude test scores is a case of the jangle fallacy: the names of the tests/constructs differ, but they actually describe the same psychological phenomenon.
  • So the difference may lie in the purpose the score is used for: predicting future performance, rather than evaluating past learning outcomes.

Two categories of aptitude test

Academic aptitude tests and special aptitude tests

  1. Academic Potential Test (TPA) — measures general cognitive capacity relevant to academic success, not tied to any one specific field of expertise.
  2. Special aptitude test — measures capacity in one particular ability domain, such as mechanical ability, clerical ability, or spatial ability.

Academic Potential Test (TPA)

Origins and general structure

  • TPA construction in Indonesia follows in part the concept behind the Graduate Record Examinations (GRE) in the United States, which divides ability into verbal reasoning, quantitative reasoning, and analytical ability (the item format of the analytical component has differed across eras — multiple choice in the 1980s, when the Indonesian TPA began to be developed, versus a written essay in the current version of the GRE).
  • The TPA in Indonesia generally consists of three subtests: Verbal, Quantitative, and Reasoning (Azwar, 2019).

Sample TPA test blueprint

Subtest Component Sample indicator
Verbal Word equivalents, antonyms, word analogies Identifying pairs of words that share the same meaning relationship
Quantitative Number series, arithmetic, algebraic concepts Identifying the underlying principle of a number series
Reasoning Logical thinking, analytical thinking Drawing a conclusion from two premises

Blueprint format adapted from the TPA specification-table tradition (Azwar, 2019).

Special aptitude tests

Measuring a single ability domain

  • Special aptitude tests are used when the decision being made concerns a single specific field, such as tracking students into vocational school programs or selecting candidates for technical job positions.
  • Commonly measured domains include: verbal ability, numerical ability, abstract reasoning, clerical speed, mechanical reasoning, and space relations.
  • A battery of aptitude tests measuring several domains at once is called a multiple aptitude battery.
    • Examples: the Differential Aptitude Tests (Bennett, Seashore, & Wesman, 1947) and the General Aptitude Test Battery, both already mentioned in Meeting 1.

Anastasi & Urbina (1997).

Why this matters for test development

Note

The same principle applies to both the TPA and special aptitude tests: the construct being measured must be defined first (Meeting 2), then translated into a blueprint that specifies the proportion of each component.

Common item formats

A few sample formats

  • Verbal analogy — “Cow relates to grass, as X relates to Y”.
  • Synonym/antonym — choosing a word with the same or opposite meaning.
  • Number series — completing a pattern in a sequence of numbers.
  • Logical reasoning — drawing a conclusion from given premises.
  • Non-verbal figural reasoning — completing a pattern in a sequence of images or shapes.

Sample number-series format

This format is commonly used in the quantitative subtest:

4, 7, 10, 13, …

  1. 15　B) 16　C) 17　D) 18

Answer key

The answer is B (16) — the pattern increases by 3 at each term. This example was created purely for illustration, not taken from any actual test, following the same principle used in Meeting 1.

Activity: classifying TPA items

For each sample item below, determine whether it belongs to the Verbal, Quantitative, or Reasoning subtest:

  1. “Choose the word most opposite in meaning to the word ‘optimistic’.”
  2. “If all A are B, and some B are C, must all A be C?”
  3. “Complete the following series: 2, 6, 18, 54, …”

Answer key

  1. Verbal. (2) Reasoning. (3) Quantitative.

Summary

  1. An aptitude test measures future learning capacity, unlike an achievement test, which measures mastery of material already taught.
  2. There are two categories of aptitude test: the Academic Potential Test (TPA) for general cognitive capacity, and special aptitude tests for a single specific ability domain.
  3. The TPA in Indonesia generally consists of three subtests — Verbal, Quantitative, and Reasoning — following in part the concept behind the GRE.
  4. Special aptitude tests are used for decisions tied to a single specific field, and can be organized as a battery measuring several domains at once.
  5. The test-development principles from Meeting 2 still apply: defining the construct and building the blueprint come before writing items.
